A survivors‑like about the end of a world

Rot Survivors

Nine gods are eating this world. You are one of many priests. Blood is the only currency, there is a finite amount of it, and every favor you buy from one god angers the gods across the wheel from it.

Horror‑adjacent, played straight. Auto‑firing weapons, a horde, a thumbstick, twenty‑odd minutes a run. Playable prototype, one file.

What you do

  1. 1

    Choose a patron. You wake at its seat holding its weapon. Two gods across the wheel are hunting you before you take a step.

  2. 2

    Take blood. Kill what comes for you and walk over what it drops. Stand in a settlement and bleed it yourself. Hunt the fat carriers and the ripe blood no god can take.

  3. 3

    Spend it at the altar on thresholds of favor in any of the nine. A weapon at the first, cards after, a passive at the fourth, and from the fifth a tithe on your enemies' blood. Every purchase raises the price of the next and shows you, before you pay, who it will anger.

  4. 4

    Live with the wheel. The gods you anger send hunters. The gods you leave alone eat the towns, carry the blood home, grow their ground, and go to war where their grounds meet. Rival priests come for the towns, and for you.

  5. 5

    Take the largest share. The run ends when you fall, when the world is eaten and everything turns to you, or when you have cast out every rival and your own god has no more use for you. The card at the end says what share of the world was yours.

What is different

The price of a choice is the shape of your other choices

Nine gods on a ring. Neighbours are allies, the far side are enemies, and the cost of favor climbs with distance from your patron and with how scattered your devotion is. The three builds that take one god from every arc are the endgame, and they anger the entire rest of the wheel.

The world is finite and everyone is eating it

Every unit of blood in a run began in a settlement. Servants bleed the towns and carry what they take home to their god's seat; the seat's ground grows with what it holds. Difficulty is not a clock; it is what the gods have eaten.

Your enemies are made of the blood you missed

Blood remembers every god it has passed through, and a god will not eat what is already its own. What you leave on the ground is taken by someone else's servant, which grows on it. A carrier three gods deep is an elite, and nothing authored it but your inefficiency.

Wrath is the cost of power, and the score is a share

Nobody is trying to kill you for its own sake. The gods are trying to eat the world, and they turn on you exactly as much as your favors deserve. Break a seat and cast a god out, and its friends rise. Break them all and your own god, with no rivals left, has no use for a priest. You win by taking more of the world than the other priests did.
